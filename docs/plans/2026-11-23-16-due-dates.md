# Plan 16: Task due dates Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A task can carry a due date, `due:: [[YYYY-MM-DD]]`, set by typing the line, from a small calendar (task
right-click menu, card menu, an editor key), and shown as a chip that turns amber two days before and red when overdue.

**Architecture:** The core reads and writes the `due::` line (one interpretation, spec §5) and hands the window the
date as `YYYY-MM-DD` (`BlockView.due`, `CardView.due`) plus an empty chip placeholder in the block's HTML. The window
alone turns a date into a label and a tone against today's local date, so the chip changes at midnight without the
core. One shared calendar popover (`DuePicker`) serves all three ways in; each passes its own way to run the command.

**Tech Stack:** Rust 1.90 (`crates/core`), Svelte 5 + TypeScript, bits-ui (Popover, ContextMenu, DropdownMenu, already
used), Vitest (node, no DOM), Playwright e2e over WebView2 (`app/e2e/smoke.mjs`). No new dependency.

**Spec:** `docs/spec.md` (binding; §5 properties and "One interpretation", §7.2 editor keys, §7.4 journal and its
date picker, §7.5 task states, §7.6 boards), `docs/format.md` (§8 properties, §9 task states, §14 history), and this
plan's "Product decisions" (the owner's, recorded in `docs/STATUS.md` under "Owner decisions in force", 2026-11-23).

## Product decisions (owner, 2026-11-23, binding)

1. The property is `due:: [[YYYY-MM-DD]]`: named "due", a date with no time, written as a journal link, so the task
   shows in that day's linked references. Breccia recognises it as it does `done::` (format §8 and §9).
2. Finishing a task keeps its `due::` line. Breccia never removes it on its own; only "Remove due date" (the owner's
   action) removes it.
3. It is set by typing the line, or by "Set due date…" (a small calendar, with "Remove due date") in a task's
   right-click menu, in the board card menu, and on a key in the editor.
4. A chip shows on board cards and next to tasks in pages and journal days (when the line is not being edited):
   "due 1 Dec", muted while the date is more than 2 days away; amber from 2 days before through the day itself ("due
   today" on the day); red "overdue 1 Dec" after it, for unfinished tasks; muted on finished tasks.
   Added by the owner, 2026-11-23: a date less than 6 days away is said in words, not as a date: "due today",
   "due tomorrow", "due in 2 days" … "due in 5 days"; from 6 days on the chip shows the date ("due 29 Nov").
5. Colours only through tokens in `app/src/styles/tokens.css`. "Today" is the local date and updates when the day
   changes while the app runs.
6. Not in this plan: sorting by due date (backlog #98), scheduled dates, repeats, reminders, natural-language dates.

## Pre-flight rulings (the planner's calls; the owner may overrule)

- R1. The due date is the first `due::` property line (key matched exactly, like `done`) whose value holds a `[[…]]`
  link to a journal name. Text after the link is ignored. `Due::` is a plain property.
- R2. "Set due date" replaces the first `due::` property line in place and removes any further ones, or adds the line
  after the last property line (the `priority::` rule, `inline::with_property`). "Remove due date" removes every
  `due::` property line. A `due::` line that is not among the property lines is plain text and never touched.
- R3. A change of state (to another state, to a finished one, removing it) and archiving leave every `due::` line as it
  is (decision 2). A block whose state is removed keeps its `due::` line; it then shows as a dimmed property, no chip.
- R4. The chip shows only on tasks. In a rendered task the property line that gives the due date is hidden (the chip
  stands for it, as the done chip does for `done::`); any further `due::` lines show dimmed.
- R5. Placement: in blocks, the due chip comes right after the task's first line, before the done chip. On a card it
  is at the end of the bottom line (after "Journal 23 Nov · created 20 Nov").
- R6. Tones: days = due − today. More than 2: muted ("due 1 Dec"); 0 to 2: amber ("due today" at 0, else "due 1 Dec");
  below 0: red ("overdue 1 Dec"). A finished task: always muted and "due 1 Dec" (also on the day or after it).
  Words (decision 4's addition): for an unfinished task, days 0 → "due today", 1 → "due tomorrow", 2 to 5 → "due in N
  days", 6 or more → "due 29 Nov". Overdue chips keep the date ("overdue 22 Nov"), and a finished task's chip always
  shows the date, since words relative to today mean nothing there. A chip said in words has the date as its tooltip
  (`title`, "1 Dec 2026"), so the date is one hover away. Tones are unchanged (amber still only from 2 days before).
- R7. The calendar opens at the task's due date (marked as chosen), else at today. "Remove due date" shows only when the
  task has a due date. "Today" moves to today, as in the go-to-a-day calendar. Esc or a click outside closes it and
  writes nothing; the focus goes back where it was (into the editor, with the caret where it was, for the key).
- R8. One undo step per pick or removal (`Ctrl Z` in the page brings the old line back).
- R9. The task right-click menu is offered in the outline of journal days and pages (the zone `LinkColorMenu` already
  wraps); a link or tag under the pointer keeps its own menu. Linked references and the open card's rows get no task
  menu in this plan (the card has its card menu).
- R10. This is format version 10: an existing `due::` line was a plain property and now reads as a due date
  (format §1's rule, like version 5 for `created::`).
- R11. Setting a due date on a selection of several blocks is not in this plan.

## Global Constraints

- Spec §2 anti-goal: never silently drop, reorder or reformat text the user wrote. Setting or removing a due date
  changes only `due::` property lines of that one block.
- Spec §5 "One interpretation": only the Rust core decides what is a due date. The window formats a date it is given.
- Breccia writes exactly `due:: [[YYYY-MM-DD]]` (no time, no text after the link).
- Files are saved with LF.
- Colours: no colour literal and no `color-mix` outside `app/src/styles/tokens.css` (a test checks it).
- Wire: a new command name keeps its dot (`block.setDue`); event names are `noun:verb`; struct field order is kept.
  After a wire type change, run `just types` (`cargo test` fails while `app/src/lib/generated.ts` is out of date).
- Logic with unit tests lives in `app/src/lib/`; `App.svelte` and components have no unit-test harness.
- Every test must be able to fail: capture a RED run before implementing.
- Commits: conventional subjects; `feat`/`fix` subjects in user words, at most about 90 characters, no file names;
  no attribution lines. Commit on `main`. (Push per the owner's instruction for the run.)
- Words from `docs/vocabulary.md`: task, card, state, property, journal day, page, board. New term: **due date**.

## Review Focus

1. **Midnight and waking from sleep.** A chip that was amber yesterday turns red after midnight, and a PC that slept
   past midnight shows the right tone on waking, without a reload. Pinned by Task 4's `Today.check` tests and Task 5's
   wiring (focus and visibility).
2. **Hand-written and odd lines.** Two `due::` lines, `due:: [[not a day]]`, `due:: [[2026-02-30]]`, `due::` after a
   plain line, `Due::`: the first valid one counts, a pick rewrites the first `due::` line and drops the further ones,
   a line that is not a property is never touched. Pinned by Task 1's tests.
3. **Typing, then the key.** Text typed in the editor just before the key is pressed must not be lost or doubled when
   the core writes the `due::` line. Pinned by Task 8's host test (the command goes through the editor's command
   path, as `Ctrl Enter` does) and e2e step 152 (types a word, presses the key, asserts the bytes).
4. **A task whose text would change the outline** (inside a fenced block) is refused like a state change, and nothing
   is written. Pinned by Task 1's `WouldChangeStructure` test.
5. **Readable in every theme.** Amber and red chip text must be legible on the page background and on a card in the
   light themes too. Pinned by Task 4's token test (contrast of at least 3:1 in every theme).
